Nature’s Masterpiece: Guiyang’s Top Scenic Spots

  1. Home
  2. Travel Blog
  3. Blog Details

Nestled in the heart of southwestern China, Guiyang, the capital of Guizhou Province, is often overshadowed by more famous Chinese metropolises like Shanghai, Beijing, or Chengdu. But for those who dare to look beyond the usual travel brochures, Guiyang reveals itself as a breathtaking canvas painted by nature itself. With its lush green mountains, misty valleys, and cascading waterfalls, this city is nothing short of a living masterpiece. In recent years, Guiyang has become a hot topic among eco-tourists and adventure seekers, not only for its natural beauty but also for its unique cultural blend and the rise of “cool tourism” — a trend that capitalizes on the city’s pleasant summer climate. Let’s dive into Guiyang’s top scenic spots that are currently lighting up travel forums and Instagram feeds.

Qianling Park: Where Urban Life Meets Wild Serenity

One of the most talked-about attractions in Guiyang right now is Qianling Park. This is not your average city park. Stretching over 400 hectares, it is a sprawling green oasis that feels like a secret forest hidden in plain sight. The park is famous for its Qianling Mountain, which offers a moderate but rewarding hike. Along the way, you’ll encounter wild macaques that have become local celebrities. These monkeys are so accustomed to human visitors that they often pose for photos or snatch a snack from an unsuspecting tourist.

The Hongfu Temple Experience

At the summit of Qianling Mountain sits the Hongfu Temple, a Buddhist sanctuary that dates back to the late 1600s. The temple is not just a spiritual site; it’s a vantage point that offers panoramic views of Guiyang’s skyline juxtaposed against rolling green hills. On a clear day, the mist lifts to reveal a city that seems to breathe in harmony with the forest. Travel bloggers have been raving about this spot for its photogenic qualities, especially during sunrise when the golden light filters through the ancient trees.

Huangguoshu Waterfall: The Thunder of Nature

No list of Guiyang’s scenic spots would be complete without mentioning Huangguoshu Waterfall, located about 45 kilometers southwest of the city. This is not just any waterfall; it is the largest waterfall in Asia, and it’s currently trending as a must-see destination for waterfall chasers and nature photographers. The waterfall spans 101 meters in width and drops 77.8 meters, creating a thunderous roar that can be heard from miles away.

The Water Curtain Cave

The real highlight here is the Water Curtain Cave, a natural tunnel that runs behind the waterfall. Walking through this cave feels like stepping into a different world. You can feel the spray of the water on your face while looking out through the cascading curtain at the surrounding cliffs and rainbows. On social media, videos of this cave experience have gone viral, with influencers describing it as “walking through a dream.”

Seasonal Variations

Huangguoshu is spectacular year-round, but the summer months (June to August) are particularly popular because the water flow is at its peak. However, the recent trend of “off-season travel” has also brought attention to the winter months, when the waterfall partially freezes, creating a surreal icy sculpture. Either way, this spot is a non-negotiable stop for anyone visiting Guiyang.

Jiaxiu Tower: A Symbol of Elegance

For those who prefer a mix of culture and scenery, Jiaxiu Tower is the place to be. Located on a large rock in the Nanming River, this three-story wooden pavilion is one of Guiyang’s most iconic landmarks. It was originally built in 1598 during the Ming Dynasty and has been rebuilt several times since. The name “Jiaxiu” means “first in talent,” reflecting the city’s historical emphasis on education and scholarship.

The Evening Spectacle

Recently, Jiaxiu Tower has become a hotspot for night photography. The tower is beautifully illuminated after sunset, and its reflection in the river creates a stunning symmetrical image. Local food vendors line the nearby Furong Street, offering everything from spicy skewers to sweet rice cakes. This area has become a favorite among vloggers who capture the vibrant street life against the backdrop of the ancient tower.

Tianhe Lake: The Hidden Gem

If you’re looking for something off the beaten path, Tianhe Lake is gaining traction among travelers who want to escape the crowds. This artificial lake, surrounded by karst hills, is known for its crystal-clear water and serene atmosphere. The lake is part of the Tianhe Lake Scenic Area, which also features a small cave system and a suspension bridge that sways gently over the water.

Kayaking and Camping

Recent travel trends have highlighted Tianhe Lake as a prime spot for kayaking and camping. The calm waters make it ideal for beginners, and the surrounding hills offer excellent hiking trails. In 2023, the area saw a surge in “glamping” setups, with luxury tents and barbecue stations popping up along the shoreline. It’s a perfect spot for those who want to reconnect with nature without sacrificing comfort.

Qingyan Ancient Town: Time Travel Through Cobblestone Streets

About 29 kilometers south of Guiyang lies Qingyan Ancient Town, a well-preserved Ming and Qing Dynasty settlement that has become a darling of history buffs and foodies alike. The town is built on a hillside, with narrow cobblestone streets winding past ancient stone houses, temples, and ancestral halls.

The Food Scene

What’s making Qingyan particularly trendy right now is its food scene. The town is famous for Qingyan tofu, a local delicacy that is crispy on the outside and soft on the inside. There’s also rose sugar, a sweet syrup made from local roses, which is used in everything from desserts to drinks. Food bloggers have been flocking here to document the traditional cooking methods and the rustic charm of the town’s eateries.

The City Wall

Another highlight is the Qingyan City Wall, which offers a panoramic view of the town and the surrounding countryside. Walking along the wall at dusk, when the lanterns are lit and the mist settles over the rooftops, is an experience that feels straight out of a historical drama. This spot has been featured in several travel documentaries recently, cementing its status as a must-visit.

Nanjiang Grand Canyon: Adrenaline and Awe

For thrill-seekers, Nanjiang Grand Canyon is the ultimate destination. Located in the Kaiyang County area, about 80 kilometers from Guiyang, this canyon is a geological wonder with steep cliffs, deep gorges, and a roaring river that cuts through the landscape. The canyon is part of the Nanjiang River Rafting experience, which has become a summer staple for locals and tourists alike.

Rafting and Zip-lining

The rafting season runs from May to October, and the rapids range from gentle to wild, catering to both beginners and experienced rafters. In addition to rafting, the canyon offers zip-lining across the gorge, which gives you a bird’s-eye view of the turquoise water below. This adrenaline-pumping activity has been trending on adventure travel websites, with many calling it “China’s answer to the Grand Canyon.”

The “Cool Tourism” Phenomenon

One of the reasons Guiyang is currently a hot topic is its summer climate. While much of China swelters in temperatures above 35°C (95°F), Guiyang enjoys a mild average of around 23°C (73°F) during the summer months. This has given rise to the concept of “cool tourism,” where travelers flock to the city to escape the heat. The local government has capitalized on this by promoting Guiyang as a “Summer Resort Capital,” complete with outdoor concerts, food festivals, and night markets that stay open late.

The Night Market Culture

Speaking of night markets, Erqi Road Snack Street is a must-visit for food lovers. This bustling market comes alive after dark, offering a dizzying array of local snacks such as sour fish soup, spicy hot pot, and rice noodles with pickled vegetables. The market is a sensory overload, with sizzling woks, fragrant spices, and the chatter of locals and tourists mingling together. It’s a perfect example of how Guiyang blends its natural beauty with vibrant urban life.

The Rise of Eco-Tourism

Another trend that has put Guiyang on the map is eco-tourism. The city is surrounded by nature reserves and protected areas, many of which have been developed with sustainable practices in mind. For instance, the Baihua Lake area has implemented strict waste management protocols and offers eco-friendly boat tours. Similarly, the Yelang Valley is a newly developed eco-park that features traditional Miao and Dong villages, where visitors can learn about indigenous cultures while supporting local communities.

The Miao and Dong Villages

Speaking of indigenous cultures, the Miao and Dong ethnic minorities are an integral part of Guiyang’s identity. Many scenic spots, such as Xijiang Qianhu Miao Village (a short drive from Guiyang), offer immersive experiences where you can stay in traditional wooden stilt houses, watch silver jewelry being made, and participate in ancient festivals. These villages have become popular on platforms like Douyin and Xiaohongshu, where short videos of colorful costumes and lively dances attract millions of views.

Practical Tips for Visiting Guiyang

Given the growing interest in Guiyang, here are some practical tips to make the most of your trip:

  • Best Time to Visit: Spring (March to May) and autumn (September to November) offer the most pleasant weather. Summer is great for escaping the heat, but expect larger crowds.
  • Getting Around: Guiyang has a modern metro system that connects most major attractions. For outlying spots like Huangguoshu, consider renting a car or joining a guided tour.
  • What to Pack: Comfortable walking shoes are a must, as many scenic spots involve hiking. A light jacket is also recommended, as evenings can be cool even in summer.
  • Language: While Mandarin is widely spoken, learning a few phrases like “Xie Xie” (thank you) can go a long way. In tourist areas, English signage is becoming more common.

The Future of Guiyang Tourism

As Guiyang continues to gain recognition, the city is investing in infrastructure to accommodate the influx of visitors. New hotels, improved roads, and better public transportation are all part of the plan. Additionally, there is a growing emphasis on digital tourism, with virtual reality experiences being developed for those who cannot visit in person. For example, the Guiyang Smart Tourism app now offers 360-degree views of major scenic spots, allowing users to explore the city from the comfort of their homes.

The Role of Social Media

Social media has played a huge role in Guiyang’s rise as a travel destination. Influencers and travel bloggers have been instrumental in showcasing the city’s beauty, often using hashtags like #GuiyangNature and #CoolGuiyang to attract attention. The city’s official tourism board has also partnered with popular platforms to host online contests and giveaways, further boosting its visibility.

A Final Thought on Nature’s Masterpiece

Guiyang is more than just a city; it is a testament to the raw, untamed beauty of nature. From the thunderous roar of Huangguoshu Waterfall to the serene stillness of Tianhe Lake, every corner of this region tells a story of harmony between humanity and the natural world. Whether you’re an adrenaline junkie, a history enthusiast, or a food lover, Guiyang offers something that will leave you in awe. And as the world continues to discover this hidden gem, one thing is certain: Guiyang is not just a destination; it is an experience that will stay with you long after you’ve returned home.
