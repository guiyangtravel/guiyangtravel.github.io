Top 10 Must-Visit Holiday Destinations in Guiyang

  1. Home
  2. Travel Blog
  3. Blog Details

Nestled in the rolling hills of Southwest China, Guiyang, the capital of Guizhou province, has long been a secret whispered among savvy travelers. Often overshadowed by its famous neighbors, this city is now emerging as a premier holiday destination, offering a breathtaking mix of surreal natural landscapes, profound ethnic culture, and a vibrant, modern urban energy. Dubbed the "Forest City" and the "Summer Capital of China" for its perpetually pleasant climate, Guiyang is an escape into a world where karst mountains pierce the mist, waterfalls thunder into emerald pools, and ancient villages hold centuries of tradition. Forget the guidebooks that only scratch the surface; this is your deep dive into the top 10 unmissable experiences that define a holiday in Guiyang.

The Natural Wonders: Where Earth Shows Its Artistry

Guiyang’s true magic lies in its otherworldly scenery, sculpted by water and time over millennia. This is a playground for nature lovers and photographers alike.

1. Qingyan Ancient Town

Step back in time just an hour from the city center at Qingyan Ancient Town. More than just a collection of old buildings, this 600-year-old Ming Dynasty fortress town is a living museum. Its gray-slated roofs and moss-covered stone walls wind up the hillside, telling tales of a strategic past. Wander through its narrow, flagstone alleys, sample local snacks like changwangmian (irresistible savory noodles) and rose sugar, and discover hidden courtyards. The blend of Han, Miao, and local cultural influences in its architecture and temples makes it a perfect first stop to understand the region's historical tapestry.

2. Huangguoshu Waterfall National Park

No trip to Guiyang is complete without witnessing the awe-inspiring power of Huangguoshu, Asia’s largest waterfall. While technically a couple of hours' drive from the city, it is the crown jewel of any Guizhou itinerary. The thunderous roar of water plunging 77.8 meters (255 feet) into the Rhinoceros Pool is a humbling sensory experience. Explore the Waterfall-Curtain Cave that allows you to walk behind the curtain of water, feeling the spray and seeing the world through a liquid lens. The surrounding park, with its smaller but equally beautiful cascades like Doupotang and Tianxing Bridge, offers a full day of breathtaking vistas.

3. Qianling Mountain Park

For a nature fix within the city limits, Qianling Mountain Park is Guiyang’s green lung. This expansive park is centered around Qianling Hill and is famous for its troops of semi-wild macaque monkeys. A hike to the top rewards you with panoramic views of the sprawling city below. Visit the Hongfu Temple, a serene Zen Buddhist monastery nestled on the mountainside. It’s a place where locals practice tai chi, elders play chess, and the harmonious coexistence of urban life and natural tranquility is on full display.

4. Tianhetan Scenic Area

Prepare to have your perception of caves completely redefined. Tianhetan is not a dark, damp cavern but a colossal, sky-lit cathedral of stone. The main chamber is one of the largest in the world, so vast that it contains its own forest and a functional 120-meter-long suspension bridge. Sunlight streams through the giant natural openings in the ceiling, illuminating the surreal rock formations and lush vegetation below. It feels less like exploring a cave and more like discovering a lost world, making it a uniquely photogenic and unforgettable stop.

Cultural Immersion: Heartbeats of the Highlands

Beyond the landscapes, the soul of Guiyang is found in the rich cultures of the ethnic groups that have called these mountains home for generations.

5. Guizhou Provincial Museum

Before venturing into the villages, arm yourself with context at the Guizhou Provincial Museum. Its striking, modern architecture is a landmark itself. Inside, world-class exhibits showcase the dazzling silver crowns and intricate embroidery of the Miao and Dong people, ancient fossils from the region, and detailed explanations of local customs and festivals. It’s a vital key to unlocking the deeper meaning behind the cultural experiences you’ll encounter throughout your holiday.

6. Xijiang Thousand Household Miao Village

While a few hours from Guiyang, Xijiang is the quintessential destination for experiencing Miao culture on a grand scale. As the world’s largest Miao settlement, its thousands of wooden stilted houses cascade down the mountainsides towards a winding river. By day, explore the labyrinth of streets, watch artisans at work, and be greeted by the sight of elders in traditional dress. As night falls, the village transforms into a sea of golden lights, with the sound of lusheng (reed pipes) and the spectacle of community dances. It’s immersive, vibrant, and profoundly beautiful.

7. Zhenyuan Ancient City

For a different cultural flavor, head to the historic canal town of Zhenyuan. Built along the winding Wuyang River, its architecture reflects a fusion of Han and minority styles. The town is split by the river, connected by ancient bridges, with the breathtaking Qinglong Cave complex clinging to the cliffs on the north side. Take a evening boat ride to see the illuminated traditional buildings reflecting on the water—a scene of timeless romance and peace.

Urban Pulse & Culinary Adventures

Guiyang’s cityscape offers its own set of delights, from trendy hubs to legendary food streets that come alive after dark.

8. Jiaxiu Tower & Nanming River

The iconic symbol of Guiyang, Jiaxiu Tower stands proudly on a giant rock in the Nanming River. This Ming Dynasty pavilion, especially when lit at night, is picture-perfect. Stroll along the renovated riverwalk, join locals for an evening walk, and feel the contemporary pulse of the city. The area seamlessly blends ancient history with modern urban planning, featuring cafes, boutiques, and public art installations.

9. Guiyang’s Night Market & Culinary Scene

Your holiday is incomplete without a deep dive into Guiyang’s legendary food. The city is a paradise for adventurous eaters. Head to the bustling Zhonghua Road or the famous "Si Li" (Flyover) snack street. Must-try dishes include: * Sour Soup Fish: The city’s signature dish—a tangy, spicy, and herb-infused broth with fresh river fish. * Changwangmian: As seen in Qingyan, these rich, savory noodles are a comfort food staple. * Siwa Wa: A beloved snack of crispy fried tofu stuffed with a spicy vegetable filling. * Beer & Barbecue: Join locals at open-air stalls for grilled meats, vegetables, and cold local beer—a quintessential Guiyang night out.

The Off-The-Beaten-Path Gem

10. Anshun Yunfeng Tunpu Villages

For a truly unique historical journey, visit the Tunpu villages near Anshun. These are not Miao or Dong settlements, but communities descended from Han Chinese soldiers sent to garrison the area 600 years ago during the Ming Dynasty. Isolated for centuries, they have preserved Ming-era customs, architecture, and even a unique form of local opera where performers wear "Dixi" masks carved from wood. Walking through villages like Tianlong or Yunshan is like stepping into a living time capsule, offering a fascinating contrast to the region's other ethnic cultures.

From the thunder of waterfalls to the delicate stitch of embroidery, from the misty peaks of ancient mountains to the sizzle of a nighttime food stall, Guiyang delivers a holiday of profound contrasts and consistent wonder. It’s a destination that engages all the senses and leaves you with memories not just of places seen, but of cultures felt and landscapes felt. Its cool climate is merely a bonus; the true warmth comes from the encounters you’ll have in this captivating corner of China.
