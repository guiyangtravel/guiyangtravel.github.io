Best Hostels in Guiyang for First-Time Visitors

  1. Home
  2. Travel Blog
  3. Blog Details

Guiyang, the capital of Guizhou Province in southwestern China, has quietly become one of the most exciting destinations for backpackers and budget travelers. Known as the "Cool City of China" due to its mild summers, Guiyang offers a unique blend of ethnic minority culture, spicy street food, and stunning karst landscapes. For first-time visitors, finding the right hostel can make or break the experience. Here is a curated list of the best hostels in Guiyang, along with tips on what to see, eat, and do while you are in this underrated gem.

Why Guiyang Should Be on Your China Travel Radar

Before diving into the hostels, it is worth understanding why Guiyang is gaining traction among savvy travelers. Unlike the mega-cities of Beijing, Shanghai, or Guangzhou, Guiyang retains a laid-back, unpolished charm. The city is surrounded by lush green mountains, and its elevation of about 1,100 meters means summer temperatures rarely exceed 30°C (86°F). This makes it a perfect escape from the oppressive heat of eastern China.

Guiyang is also the gateway to Guizhou's incredible natural wonders, such as the Huangguoshu Waterfall, the largest waterfall in Asia, and the Miao and Dong ethnic minority villages in the surrounding countryside. For food lovers, the local cuisine is a fiery adventure, with dishes like sour fish soup (suan tang yu), spicy beef offal, and the infamous "stinky" tofu. The city's vibrant night markets, such as Erqi Road, are a must-visit for any foodie.

In recent years, Guiyang has also positioned itself as a tech hub, with a growing digital economy and a reputation as China's "Big Data Valley." This has brought a wave of young entrepreneurs and digital nomads, adding a modern, cosmopolitan vibe to the city's traditional roots. All of this makes Guiyang an ideal destination for first-time visitors looking for an authentic, off-the-beaten-path experience.

What to Look for in a Hostel in Guiyang

For first-time visitors, choosing a hostel in Guiyang requires some consideration. Here are key factors to keep in mind:

  • Location: The city center, around the Zhongshan Road and Dashizi areas, is the most convenient for accessing major attractions, night markets, and public transport. Hostels near the Jiaxiu Tower or the Nanming River offer scenic views and easy walking access to the old town.
  • Social Atmosphere: Many hostels in Guiyang cater to a mix of Chinese and international travelers. Look for places with common areas, rooftop terraces, or organized activities like family dinners or hiking trips.
  • Cleanliness and Comfort: Given the humid subtropical climate, air conditioning and good ventilation are essential. Also, check for private curtain bunks, lockers, and reliable Wi-Fi.
  • Local Knowledge: The best hostels have staff who can recommend hidden gems, help you book tours to ethnic villages, or teach you how to navigate the local bus system.

Top Hostels in Guiyang for First-Time Visitors

1. Guiyang Mingdu International Youth Hostel

Located in the heart of the city, just a 5-minute walk from the Jiaxiu Tower and the bustling Erqi Road Night Market, the Mingdu International Youth Hostel is a favorite among backpackers. The hostel occupies a traditional Chinese building with a charming courtyard, where guests can relax under the shade of a banyan tree.

Why It Stands Out: The Mingdu has a genuinely social vibe. The common room is spacious, with a pool table, board games, and a bookshelf filled with travel guides. The staff organizes weekly dumpling-making nights and hiking trips to nearby mountains. The dorm beds are comfortable, with thick mattresses and privacy curtains. Private rooms are also available for couples or solo travelers seeking more space.

Nearby Attractions: From here, you can walk to the Jiaxiu Tower, a Ming Dynasty pavilion that is the symbol of Guiyang. The Qianling Park, a sprawling forested area with a zoo and a Buddhist temple, is a 20-minute bus ride away. For food, the Erqi Road Night Market is a sensory overload of grilled skewers, fried rice noodles, and sweet tofu pudding.

Price Range: Dorm beds start at around 50 RMB per night, while private rooms range from 150 to 250 RMB.

2. Guiyang Wow Hostel

If you are looking for a more modern, design-forward experience, the Guiyang Wow Hostel is a great choice. Located in the trendy Huaguoyuan area, this hostel is part of a new wave of boutique hostels that blend industrial chic with Chinese aesthetics. The lobby features exposed brick walls, hanging plants, and a neon sign that reads "Wow."

Why It Stands Out: The Wow Hostel is all about comfort and convenience. Each dorm bed comes with its own reading light, USB port, and a personal locker that is large enough for a 60-liter backpack. The bathrooms are spotless, with powerful showers and complimentary toiletries. The hostel also has a rooftop bar that serves craft beer and cocktails, with views of the city skyline.

Social Scene: The Wow Hostel attracts a young, international crowd. There is a co-working space on the second floor, perfect for digital nomads, and the staff organizes pub crawls and karaoke nights. The hostel also offers free walking tours of the Huaguoyuan area, which is known for its street art and indie cafes.

Nearby Attractions: Huaguoyuan is a vibrant neighborhood with a mix of traditional markets and modern shopping malls. The Guiyang Museum, which showcases the history and culture of Guizhou's ethnic minorities, is a 15-minute walk away. For a taste of local life, visit the Huaxi Night Market, where you can try "silk" tofu and grilled fish.

Price Range: Dorm beds are slightly higher, around 70 to 90 RMB, but the quality justifies the cost. Private rooms start at 200 RMB.

3. Guiyang Backpacker Hostel

For travelers on a tight budget, the Guiyang Backpacker Hostel offers the best value in the city. Located near the Guiyang Railway Station, this no-frills hostel is ideal for those who plan to use Guiyang as a base for exploring the surrounding countryside. The hostel is housed in a converted apartment building, with a simple but functional layout.

Why It Stands Out: The Backpacker Hostel is all about affordability and practicality. Dorm beds are among the cheapest in the city, and the hostel provides free luggage storage, a shared kitchen, and a laundry service. The staff are incredibly helpful, often going out of their way to help guests book bus tickets to Huangguoshu Waterfall or the Miao villages.

Atmosphere: The vibe here is more low-key compared to the other hostels. The common area is small but cozy, with a TV and a few couches. Many guests are Chinese backpackers, so it is a good place to practice Mandarin and learn about domestic travel routes. The hostel also has a bulletin board where travelers post notes about shared taxis or hiking groups.

Nearby Attractions: The hostel is a 10-minute walk from the railway station, making it easy to catch trains to other cities like Kunming or Chengdu. The nearby Hebin Park is a peaceful spot for a morning jog, and the Guiyang No. 1 Night Market is a short bus ride away.

Price Range: Dorm beds start at just 35 RMB, making it one of the cheapest options in the city. Private rooms are available for around 100 RMB.

4. Guiyang Riverside Hostel

As the name suggests, the Riverside Hostel is situated along the Nanming River, offering stunning views of the water and the surrounding hills. This hostel is a bit further from the city center, but the peaceful location more than makes up for it. The building is a renovated villa with a garden terrace where guests can enjoy breakfast or evening drinks.

Why It Stands Out: The Riverside Hostel is perfect for travelers who want a quiet retreat after a day of sightseeing. The rooms are spacious and airy, with large windows that let in natural light. The hostel also has a small library with books on Guizhou's history and culture, as well as a collection of board games.

Activities: The hostel organizes bike rentals and guided cycling tours along the river. In the summer, guests can go kayaking or paddleboarding on the Nanming. The staff also runs a weekly barbecue night, where guests can grill local meats and vegetables while sharing travel stories.

Nearby Attractions: The Riverside Hostel is close to the Guizhou Provincial Museum, which houses an impressive collection of bronze drums and traditional costumes. The Qianling Park is a 30-minute walk away, but the scenic route along the river makes it a pleasant stroll.

Price Range: Dorm beds are around 60 RMB, while private rooms with river views start at 180 RMB.

5. Guiyang Art Hostel

For creative travelers and art lovers, the Guiyang Art Hostel is a hidden gem. Located in a converted factory in the Wudang district, this hostel is a collaboration between local artists and designers. The walls are covered with murals, and the furniture is made from recycled materials. The hostel also hosts regular art exhibitions, poetry readings, and live music performances.

Why It Stands Out: The Art Hostel is more than just a place to sleep; it is a cultural hub. The common area doubles as a gallery, with rotating displays of contemporary Chinese art. The hostel offers workshops in calligraphy, paper-cutting, and batik printing, giving guests a hands-on introduction to Guizhou's traditional crafts.

Atmosphere: The crowd here is eclectic, with a mix of artists, musicians, and travelers. The hostel has a relaxed, bohemian vibe, with hammocks in the courtyard and a rooftop garden where guests can meditate or do yoga. The staff are passionate about local culture and can recommend off-the-beaten-path attractions, such as the Qingyan Ancient Town or the Tianlong Tunpu, a Ming Dynasty fortress village.

Nearby Attractions: The Wudang district is known for its street art and indie cafes. The Guiyang Art Museum is a 15-minute walk away, and the nearby Wudang Mountain offers hiking trails with panoramic views of the city.

Price Range: Dorm beds are around 80 RMB, and private rooms start at 220 RMB. The hostel also offers artist residencies for longer stays.

Tips for First-Time Visitors to Guiyang

Getting Around Guiyang

Guiyang has a modern metro system with two lines that connect the main train station, the airport, and key attractions. Taxis are affordable, with a base fare of around 8 RMB, but be prepared for traffic jams during rush hour. For a more local experience, try the electric scooters (dian dong che) that zip through the city's narrow alleys.

Must-Try Foods in Guiyang

Guiyang's food scene is a highlight of any visit. Here are a few dishes you cannot miss:

  • Sour Fish Soup (Suan Tang Yu): A tangy, spicy broth made with fermented tomatoes and fresh river fish. It is a staple of Guizhou cuisine.
  • Chang Wang Noodles: A hearty bowl of beef offal noodles, served with a rich, peppery broth.
  • Stinky Tofu (Chou Doufu): Despite its pungent smell, this deep-fried tofu is crispy on the outside and soft on the inside, often served with a chili dip.
  • Guiyang Spicy Chicken (Guiyang La Zi Ji): A fiery stir-fry of chicken, dried chilies, and Sichuan peppercorns.
  • Rice Tofu (Mi Doufu): A sweet, jelly-like dessert made from rice flour, often topped with brown sugar syrup and crushed peanuts.

Day Trips from Guiyang

Guiyang is the perfect base for exploring Guizhou's countryside. Popular day trips include:

  • Huangguoshu Waterfall: A 2-hour bus ride from the city, this is the largest waterfall in Asia and a must-see for nature lovers.
  • Xijiang Qianhu Miao Village: The largest Miao village in China, known for its terraced rice fields and traditional wooden houses.
  • Qingyan Ancient Town: A well-preserved Ming Dynasty town with cobblestone streets and ancient temples.
  • Tianlong Tunpu: A fortress village built by Ming Dynasty soldiers, famous for its stone houses and unique "dixi" (ground opera) performances.

Cultural Etiquette

Guiyang is a multicultural city, with significant Miao, Dong, and Buyi populations. When visiting ethnic minority villages, dress modestly, ask for permission before taking photos, and avoid touching sacred objects. In the city, locals are generally friendly and welcoming, but learning a few phrases in Mandarin, such as "xie xie" (thank you) and "duo shao qian" (how much), will go a long way.

Final Thoughts on Choosing a Hostel in Guiyang

Guiyang is a city that rewards those who take the time to explore its hidden corners. Whether you choose the social buzz of the Mingdu, the modern comforts of the Wow, or the artistic flair of the Art Hostel, you will find a welcoming community of fellow travelers and helpful staff. The key is to match your hostel choice with your travel style. If you are a solo traveler looking to make friends, go for a hostel with a strong social scene. If you need a quiet place to work or recharge, opt for a more laid-back option like the Riverside Hostel.

No matter where you stay, Guiyang will leave a lasting impression. Its cool climate, spicy food, and warm hospitality make it a standout destination in China. So pack your bags, book a bed, and get ready to discover one of the country's best-kept secrets.
