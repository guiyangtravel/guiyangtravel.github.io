Guiyang’s Famous Landmarks: A Traveler’s Bucket List

  1. Home
  2. Travel Blog
  3. Blog Details

Nestled in the heart of Southwest China, Guiyang is often overshadowed by its more famous neighbors like Chengdu and Kunming. But for the savvy traveler, this capital of Guizhou Province is a hidden gem waiting to be uncovered. With its lush green mountains, unique karst landscapes, and a rich tapestry of ethnic minority cultures, Guiyang offers an authentic slice of China that feels both ancient and refreshingly modern. Whether you're a nature lover, a history buff, or a foodie looking for your next obsession, Guiyang’s landmarks are the kind of bucket-list destinations that will leave you breathless—literally, if you’re not used to the altitude. So pack your bags, grab your hiking boots, and let’s dive into the must-see spots that make Guiyang a traveler’s paradise.

Jiaxiu Tower: The Soul of the City

Let’s start with the icon that defines Guiyang’s skyline: Jiaxiu Tower. Built in 1598 during the Ming Dynasty, this three-story wooden pagoda stands proudly on a large rock jutting out from the Nanming River. The name “Jiaxiu” translates to “The First Scholar,” a nod to the city’s historical emphasis on education and culture. Locals will tell you that the tower is the heart of Guiyang, and they’re not wrong.

Why It’s a Must-See

Jiaxiu Tower isn’t just a pretty face; it’s a symbol of resilience. The structure has been destroyed and rebuilt multiple times due to wars and floods, yet it remains a testament to the city’s enduring spirit. As you walk across the stone bridge to reach the tower, you’ll notice the intricate carvings and the delicate upturned eaves that seem to reach for the sky. The surrounding park is a favorite spot for locals practicing Tai Chi in the morning or playing cards under the shade of ancient banyan trees.

Tips for Visiting

  • Best Time to Go: Early morning or late afternoon to avoid crowds and catch the golden hour light.
  • What to Do: Climb to the top for panoramic views of the city and the river. The entrance fee is a mere 20 RMB (about 3 USD).
  • Pro Tip: Pair your visit with a stroll along the nearby Fuqiang Road, where you can find street food stalls selling siwawa (Guiyang-style spring rolls) and changwang mian (intestine noodles). Yes, it’s as adventurous as it sounds.

Qianling Park: Where Nature Meets Spirituality

If you need a break from the urban hustle, Qianling Park is your sanctuary. This sprawling green space covers over 4 square kilometers and is home to a mix of natural beauty and cultural landmarks. The park is named after Qianling Mountain, which is considered a sacred site in Guizhou’s Buddhist tradition.

The Hongfu Temple

At the heart of the park lies Hongfu Temple, a Buddhist monastery that dates back to the 17th century. The temple is a serene escape, with incense smoke curling around ancient statues and the sound of monks chanting in the background. The architecture is classic Ming and Qing style, with red pillars and golden roofs that contrast beautifully with the surrounding greenery. Don’t miss the giant Buddha statue carved into the mountain—it’s a sight that inspires awe, even for non-religious visitors.

The Qianling Lake

For a more leisurely experience, head to Qianling Lake. You can rent a paddleboat or simply sit on a bench and watch the koi fish swim lazily in the crystal-clear water. The lake is surrounded by walking trails that lead to hidden waterfalls and caves. If you’re lucky, you might spot a few monkeys swinging through the trees—just don’t feed them, as they’ve been known to get a bit aggressive.

Hiking Trails

For the adventurous, Qianling Park offers several hiking trails that climb up the mountain. The most popular route takes about 45 minutes and rewards you with a stunning view of the entire city from the summit. The trail is steep in places, but the fresh air and the sense of accomplishment make it worth the effort.

Huaxi National Wetland Park: A Green Oasis

Just a short drive from the city center, Huaxi National Wetland Park is a masterpiece of ecological restoration. This park was once a polluted industrial area, but it has been transformed into a lush wetland that serves as a habitat for hundreds of bird species and plants. It’s a testament to China’s growing commitment to environmental conservation.

What to Expect

The park is divided into several zones, each with its own character. The most popular is the “Flower Sea” area, where fields of lavender, sunflowers, and lotuses bloom in stunning patterns. There’s also a boardwalk that winds through the marsh, allowing you to get up close to the reeds and water lilies. The park is designed with sustainability in mind, using solar-powered lights and rainwater collection systems.

Activities for Families

Huaxi is a great spot for families. There’s a children’s playground, a butterfly museum, and even a small farm where kids can feed goats and rabbits. You can also rent a bike or a tandem cart to explore the park’s 20 kilometers of cycling paths. If you’re visiting in summer, don’t miss the lotus pond—it’s a photographer’s dream.

Local Flavor

Near the park entrance, you’ll find a food court serving Guizhou specialties. Try the suan tang yu (sour soup fish), a local dish that uses fermented tomatoes to create a tangy, spicy broth. It’s a flavor explosion that will make your taste buds dance.

Qingyan Ancient Town: A Step Back in Time

If you’re craving history, Qingyan Ancient Town is the place to be. Located about 30 kilometers from Guiyang, this well-preserved Ming and Qing dynasty town is a living museum of traditional Chinese architecture and culture. The town is built on a hillside, with cobblestone streets winding between ancient stone houses, temples, and ancestral halls.

The Town’s History

Qingyan was once a strategic military outpost and a key stop on the ancient Tea Horse Road. Its walls and gates were designed to withstand attacks, and the layout of the town is a maze of narrow alleys and hidden courtyards. As you walk through the main gate, you’ll feel like you’ve stepped into a different era.

Must-See Sights

  • The Ancient City Wall: Climb the wall for a bird’s-eye view of the town and the surrounding countryside.
  • Ding Guang Gate: The main entrance to the town, with its massive wooden doors and iron studs.
  • Zhao Family Mansion: A beautifully preserved residence that offers a glimpse into the life of a wealthy merchant family.
  • The Temple of the City God: A Taoist temple with intricate carvings and a peaceful courtyard.

Food and Shopping

Qingyan is famous for its yuanxiao (glutinous rice balls) and la rou (cured pork). You can also find handmade crafts like batik textiles and bamboo carvings. The town is less touristy than other ancient towns in China, so you’ll get a more authentic experience. Bargaining is expected, but be respectful—the vendors are locals who rely on tourism for their livelihood.

The Guizhou Provincial Museum: A Cultural Treasure Trove

To truly understand Guiyang, you need to dive into its cultural roots, and the Guizhou Provincial Museum is the perfect place to start. This modern museum houses an impressive collection of artifacts from the province’s 49 ethnic minority groups, including the Miao, Dong, and Buyi.

The Exhibits

The museum is divided into several galleries, each focusing on a different aspect of Guizhou’s heritage. The highlight is the “Silver Ornaments of the Miao” exhibit, which showcases the intricate silver jewelry worn by Miao women during festivals. The craftsmanship is mind-blowing—each piece is handcrafted with symbols that represent fertility, prosperity, and protection.

Interactive Experiences

One of the coolest features of the museum is the interactive zone, where you can try on traditional costumes, learn how to weave batik fabric, or even play a lusheng (a bamboo mouth organ). It’s a great way to engage with the culture in a hands-on way.

Practical Info

  • Location: No. 168 Beijing Road, Guiyang.
  • Hours: 9:00 AM to 5:00 PM (closed on Mondays).
  • Admission: Free, but you’ll need to bring your passport for entry.

The Night Market: A Feast for the Senses

No trip to Guiyang is complete without experiencing its legendary night market. The city comes alive after dark, with food stalls, street performers, and vendors selling everything from electronics to antiques. The most famous night market is on Erqi Road, but you’ll find smaller ones scattered throughout the city.

What to Eat

Guiyang’s street food is legendary. Here are some must-try items:

  • Huaxi Beef Noodles: A spicy noodle soup with tender beef and a broth that’s been simmered for hours.
  • Kao Tofu: Grilled tofu skewers brushed with chili oil and cumin.
  • Zha Huangque: Deep-fried sparrows—yes, you read that right. They’re crispy, salty, and surprisingly addictive.
  • Mango Sticky Rice: A sweet ending to your meal, with fresh mango and coconut milk.

The Vibe

The night market is a sensory overload—bright neon lights, the sizzle of woks, the chatter of locals, and the smell of grilled meat and spices. It’s chaotic, loud, and absolutely magical. Don’t be shy; grab a plastic stool, order a cold beer, and dive into the madness.

The Karst Caves: Nature’s Underground Art

Guiyang is surrounded by karst landscapes, and the region is home to some of the most spectacular caves in China. The most famous is the Zhijin Cave, located about 100 kilometers from the city, but there are smaller caves closer to town that are worth a visit.

Zhijin Cave

Zhijin Cave is a massive underground labyrinth that stretches for over 12 kilometers. Inside, you’ll find towering stalactites, crystal-clear underground rivers, and caverns the size of football fields. The cave is illuminated with colorful lights that highlight the natural formations, creating a surreal, otherworldly atmosphere.

Tianlong Cave

For a more accessible option, check out Tianlong Cave, which is only about 30 minutes from Guiyang. This cave is smaller but equally impressive, with a series of waterfalls and pools that reflect the light like mirrors. There’s also a glass-bottom boat ride that takes you through the cave’s underground river—a thrilling experience for those who aren’t afraid of heights.

Tips for Cave Exploration

  • Wear Comfortable Shoes: The cave floors can be slippery, and you’ll be doing a lot of walking.
  • Bring a Jacket: Caves are cool and damp, even in summer.
  • Hire a Guide: The caves are massive, and it’s easy to get lost without a guide.

The Miao and Dong Villages: A Cultural Immersion

To truly experience Guiyang’s ethnic diversity, you need to venture into the surrounding countryside. The Miao and Dong villages are scattered across the hills, each with its own unique customs and traditions.

Xijiang Qianhu Miao Village

Xijiang is the largest Miao village in China, with over 1,000 households. The village is built on a hillside, with wooden stilt houses that seem to stack on top of each other. At night, the village lights up like a Christmas tree, creating a breathtaking view from the observation deck.

Zhaoxing Dong Village

Zhaoxing is a Dong village known for its drum towers and wind-and-rain bridges. The Dong people are famous for their polyphonic choir singing, which is recognized by UNESCO as an intangible cultural heritage. If you’re lucky, you might catch a performance in the village square.

How to Visit

You can join a guided tour from Guiyang, or take a bus to the villages on your own. Most villages have guesthouses where you can stay overnight, giving you a chance to experience the local way of life. Be respectful of the customs—ask before taking photos, and dress modestly.

The Guiyang Food Scene: Beyond the Noodles

Guiyang’s food scene is a reflection of its multicultural population. The cuisine is known for its bold flavors—sour, spicy, and numbing—thanks to the heavy use of chili peppers, fermented ingredients, and Sichuan peppercorns.

Signature Dishes

  • Siyue Gui: A cold dish made with shredded chicken, cucumber, and a spicy sesame sauce.
  • Laobing: A thick, chewy pancake that’s often stuffed with pork and vegetables.
  • Suantang Yu: As mentioned earlier, this sour soup fish is a Guiyang staple. The broth is made with fermented tomatoes and wild peppers, giving it a unique tangy kick.

Where to Eat

For an authentic experience, head to the “Old Street” area near Jiaxiu Tower. There are dozens of small restaurants and food stalls here, each with its own specialty. Don’t be afraid to try something new—you might discover your new favorite dish.

Practical Tips for Travelers

Before you go, here are a few practical tips to make your trip smoother:

  • Getting There: Guiyang Longdongbao International Airport has direct flights from major Chinese cities like Beijing, Shanghai, and Guangzhou. High-speed trains also connect Guiyang to Chengdu, Chongqing, and Kunming.
  • Best Time to Visit: Spring (March to May) and autumn (September to November) are the best seasons, with mild temperatures and clear skies. Summer is hot and rainy, while winter can be chilly and damp.
  • Language: Mandarin is widely spoken, but English is not common. Download a translation app or learn a few basic phrases like “ni hao” (hello) and “xie xie” (thank you).
  • Currency: The Chinese yuan (RMB) is the only accepted currency. Credit cards are accepted in larger hotels and restaurants, but cash is king at street markets and smaller shops.
  • Safety: Guiyang is generally safe for tourists, but watch out for pickpockets in crowded areas. Keep your valuables secure and avoid walking alone late at night in unfamiliar neighborhoods.

Guiyang is a city that rewards the curious traveler. It’s not as polished as Shanghai or as historic as Xi’an, but it has a raw, unpretentious charm that’s hard to find elsewhere. From the ancient streets of Qingyan to the neon-lit chaos of the night market, every corner of this city tells a story. So go ahead—add Guiyang to your bucket list. You won’t regret it.
