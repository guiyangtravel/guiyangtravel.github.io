Guiyang’s Best Spots for Stargazing

  1. Home
  2. Travel Blog
  3. Blog Details

Guiyang, the capital of Guizhou Province in southwest China, is often celebrated for its lush green mountains, misty karst landscapes, and a climate that keeps the city cool even in summer. But what many travelers overlook is its potential as a stargazing destination. While Guiyang itself is a bustling urban center with light pollution, the surrounding countryside offers some of the most pristine night skies in southern China. Whether you are a seasoned astronomer with a telescope or a casual traveler seeking a romantic evening under the stars, Guiyang’s outskirts have spots that will leave you breathless. In this guide, I will walk you through the best locations for stargazing near Guiyang, what to expect in terms of weather and timing, and how to make the most of your celestial adventure.

Why Guiyang for Stargazing?

You might wonder why Guiyang, a city known for its frequent drizzle and overcast skies, is recommended for stargazing. The secret lies in its geography and altitude. Guiyang sits at an elevation of around 1,100 meters (3,600 feet), which places it above much of the low-lying haze that obscures stars in other parts of China. The surrounding mountains, such as the Qianling Mountains and the Wumeng Mountain range, create natural barriers against urban light pollution. Moreover, the best stargazing happens during the dry season from October to March, when the skies clear up significantly. During these months, the air is crisp, and the humidity drops, making the stars appear sharper and brighter. For those willing to venture a bit further, the rural areas of Guizhou offer some of the darkest skies in the region, rivaling even remote observatories.

Key Factors for Stargazing in Guiyang

Before diving into specific spots, it is important to understand the local conditions. The weather in Guiyang is famously unpredictable, even during the dry season. Always check the forecast before heading out, and be prepared for sudden fog or light rain. Light pollution is another factor. While the city center is bright, places like Huaxi District or the outskirts of Qingzhen are significantly darker. The moon phase also matters; a new moon or a crescent moon provides the darkest skies. Finally, altitude plays a role. Higher elevations not only reduce atmospheric interference but also offer panoramic views of the horizon.

Top Stargazing Spots Around Guiyang

Huaxi National Wetland Park

Located about 17 kilometers south of Guiyang’s city center, Huaxi National Wetland Park is a popular escape for locals and tourists alike. During the day, it is famous for its serene lakes, bamboo groves, and ancient bridges. But after sunset, the park transforms into a quiet sanctuary for stargazers. The park’s large open spaces, such as the lawn near the Pearl Spring, provide unobstructed views of the sky. Because it is a protected area, artificial lighting is minimal, especially in the deeper sections away from the main paths.

Best Time to Visit: October to December, when the weather is driest and the air is clearest. Arrive by 8 PM to allow your eyes to adjust to the darkness.

What to Bring: A blanket or a reclining chair, a red flashlight to preserve night vision, and a star map app on your phone. Binoculars are sufficient for viewing the Milky Way, but a small telescope will reveal Jupiter’s moons or Saturn’s rings.

Tips: The park closes at 10 PM, so plan your visit accordingly. If you want to stay longer, consider camping at one of the designated sites nearby, though you will need a permit. The park is also home to fireflies in the summer, adding a magical touch to the experience.

Qianling Mountain Park

Qianling Mountain Park is one of Guiyang’s most iconic landmarks, known for its lush forests and the Hongfu Temple. While the park is popular during the day, few people realize that the mountain’s summit offers one of the best elevated stargazing spots within the city limits. The peak, called Guanyin Peak, stands at about 1,300 meters (4,265 feet) and provides a 360-degree view of the surrounding landscape. On a clear night, you can see the city lights far below, but the sky above remains remarkably dark due to the mountain’s elevation.

Best Time to Visit: Late autumn and winter, when the air is dry and the leaves have fallen, opening up the horizon. Avoid weekends, as the park can be crowded during the day, and the trails are less safe after dark.

What to Bring: Warm clothing, as the temperature drops significantly at the summit. A thermos of hot tea or coffee is a good idea. A tripod for your camera is essential if you want to capture long-exposure shots of the stars.

Tips: The park officially closes at 6 PM, but the summit area is accessible via a side trail that does not require a ticket. However, be cautious of loose rocks and slippery paths. For safety, go with a group and inform someone of your plans. The Hongfu Temple occasionally hosts night-time meditation sessions, which can be combined with stargazing for a unique spiritual experience.

Qingzhen City and the Hongfeng Lake Area

About 30 kilometers west of Guiyang, Qingzhen City is a small urban center surrounded by karst hills and reservoirs. The Hongfeng Lake, also known as Red Maple Lake, is a popular tourist spot during the day, but its remote islands and peninsulas become ideal stargazing locations at night. The lake’s vast water surface reflects the stars, creating a surreal mirror effect. Several guesthouses and homestays around the lake offer rooftop terraces with unobstructed views, making it easy to spend the entire night under the sky.

Best Time to Visit: The dry season from November to February. The lake is less crowded during these months, and the water is calm, which enhances the reflection.

What to Bring: A waterproof mat or a hammock, as the ground near the lake can be damp. A star tracker app is helpful for identifying constellations. If you are using a camera, a wide-angle lens with a fast aperture (f/1.8 or lower) is ideal for capturing the Milky Way.

Tips: The area around Hongfeng Lake is known for its clear skies, but be aware of fog that can roll in from the water. Check the wind direction before setting up. Some local farmers offer boat rides to the islands at night for a small fee. This is a fantastic way to reach the darkest spots, but negotiate the price beforehand. Also, bring insect repellent, as mosquitoes can be active even in cooler months.

The Wumeng Mountain Range (Near Shuicheng)

For serious stargazers willing to travel a bit further, the Wumeng Mountain Range, located about 150 kilometers northwest of Guiyang, is a hidden gem. This area is sparsely populated, with small villages like Shuicheng (now part of Liupanshui) serving as gateways. The mountains here reach elevations of over 2,000 meters (6,560 feet), placing them well above the inversion layer that traps pollution. The result is a sky that is so dark that the Milky Way appears as a vivid, three-dimensional river of light. Several local guesthouses cater to astro-tourists, offering rooftop observatories and even basic telescopes for rent.

Best Time to Visit: The winter months from December to February, when the skies are at their clearest. Summer is also good, but the monsoon season can bring unexpected storms.

What to Bring: A high-quality telescope or binoculars, a portable power bank for your devices, and a sleeping bag if you plan to camp. The temperature can drop below freezing at night, so thermal underwear and a down jacket are essential.

Tips: The Wumeng Mountains are remote, so plan your transportation in advance. Renting a car from Guiyang is the most convenient option, but public buses to Shuicheng are available. Once there, hire a local guide to take you to the best spots, as the terrain can be challenging. The village of Hailongtun, known for its ancient fortress ruins, is a particularly good location for stargazing due to its high vantage point and lack of artificial light.

The Guizhou Pingtang International Experience Zone

Located about 200 kilometers south of Guiyang, the Pingtang area is famous for the Five-hundred-meter Aperture Spherical Telescope (FAST), the world’s largest radio telescope. While the telescope itself is off-limits to tourists at night, the surrounding Pingtang International Experience Zone has been developed as an astro-tourism destination. The zone includes a science museum, a planetarium, and several observation decks designed for stargazing. The entire area is a “radio quiet zone,” meaning that all electronic devices, including cell phones, must be turned off or kept in shielded bags. This ensures an incredibly dark and interference-free environment.

Best Time to Visit: Any time of the year, but the winter months provide the most stable weather. The zone is open to visitors during the day, but night visits require a special permit obtained through a tour operator.

What to Bring: Since electronic devices are restricted, bring a paper star chart and a notebook for sketching. A manual telescope without digital components is allowed. The zone provides shielded bags for phones, but it is better to leave them in your hotel.

Tips: This is the most unique stargazing experience near Guiyang, but it is also the most regulated. Book your visit at least two weeks in advance, as slots are limited. The science museum offers guided night tours that explain the science behind radio astronomy, making it an educational trip as well. Be prepared for a long drive, but the pristine skies are worth the effort.

Practical Tips for Stargazing in Guiyang

Timing and Weather

The best months for stargazing are October through March, with January and February offering the driest conditions. However, even during these months, the weather can change rapidly. Use apps like Windy or AccuWeather to check cloud cover and humidity levels. The ideal conditions are clear skies with less than 20% cloud cover and humidity below 60%. Avoid nights with a full moon, as its brightness washes out fainter stars.

Gear and Preparation

You do not need expensive equipment to enjoy stargazing in Guiyang. A pair of binoculars (7x50 or 10x50) is sufficient for most purposes. If you are using a camera, a tripod is essential for long exposures. For smartphone users, apps like NightCap or ProCamera can help capture decent shots of the Milky Way. Dress in layers, as temperatures can drop significantly after sunset. Bring snacks and water, as most stargazing spots are far from restaurants.

Safety Considerations

Many of these spots are in rural areas with uneven terrain. Always let someone know your location and expected return time. Carry a first-aid kit and a fully charged phone. If you are camping, choose a site that is at least 100 meters away from any water source to avoid condensation. In the Wumeng Mountains, be aware of wild boars and other wildlife; store food in sealed containers.

Cultural Etiquette

Guizhou is home to many ethnic minorities, including the Miao, Dong, and Buyi peoples. If you visit villages near stargazing spots, be respectful of local customs. Some communities hold night-time festivals or rituals that may involve bonfires or music. Ask for permission before setting up equipment on private land. A small gift, such as fruit or tea, is always appreciated.

Combining Stargazing with Local Tourism

Stargazing does not have to be a standalone activity. Many of these spots are near popular tourist attractions. For example, after a night of stargazing at Huaxi National Wetland Park, you can spend the next day exploring the Qingyan Ancient Town, a well-preserved Ming Dynasty settlement. Similarly, the Hongfeng Lake area is close to the Zhijin Cave, one of China’s largest karst caves. By combining stargazing with daytime tourism, you can create a well-rounded itinerary that showcases the best of Guizhou’s natural and cultural heritage.

A Sample 3-Day Itinerary

Day 1: Arrive in Guiyang, visit Qianling Mountain Park during the day, and hike to the summit for stargazing at night. Stay at a hotel near the park.

Day 2: Drive to Qingzhen, explore Hongfeng Lake in the afternoon, and set up for stargazing on one of the lake’s islands. Stay at a lakeside guesthouse.

Day 3: Head to the Wumeng Mountains or Pingtang, depending on your interest. Spend the night stargazing and return to Guiyang the next morning.

This itinerary balances adventure with relaxation, allowing you to experience different types of landscapes and sky conditions.

Final Thoughts on Guiyang’s Night Skies

Guiyang and its surroundings offer a surprising diversity of stargazing opportunities, from urban parks to remote mountain ranges. The key is to plan ahead, respect the environment, and embrace the unpredictability of the weather. Whether you are capturing the Milky Way over Hongfeng Lake or listening to the silence of the Wumeng Mountains, the experience will stay with you long after you leave. The night sky in Guizhou is not just a scientific phenomenon; it is a reminder of the vastness of the universe and the beauty of our own planet. So pack your bags, grab your telescope, and head to Guiyang for an unforgettable celestial journey.
