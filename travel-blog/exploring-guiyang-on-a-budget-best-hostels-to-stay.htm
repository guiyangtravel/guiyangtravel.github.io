Exploring Guiyang on a Budget: Best Hostels to Stay

  1. Home
  2. Travel Blog
  3. Blog Details

Guiyang, the capital of Guizhou province, has quietly become one of China’s most underrated travel destinations. Nestled in the mountainous southwest, this city offers a unique blend of natural beauty, vibrant street food culture, and a laid-back atmosphere that stands in stark contrast to the hyper-paced metropolises of Beijing or Shanghai. For budget travelers, Guiyang is a goldmine. The cost of living is low, the local cuisine is incredibly affordable, and the city serves as a perfect gateway to explore the stunning karst landscapes, rice terraces, and minority villages of Guizhou. But before you dive into the spicy sour fish soup or hike the waterfalls of Huangguoshu, you need a place to crash. And not just any place—a hostel that balances cost, comfort, and community.

In this guide, we’ll walk through the best hostels in Guiyang for budget-conscious travelers, covering everything from location and atmosphere to amenities and hidden gems. Whether you’re a solo backpacker looking to meet fellow travelers or a digital nomad needing a quiet corner to work, these hostels have you covered.

Why Guiyang Deserves a Spot on Your Bucket List

Before we get into the nitty-gritty of hostel recommendations, let’s talk about why Guiyang is trending right now. In 2024 and 2025, Guizhou province has seen a surge in domestic tourism, driven by social media trends and a growing appreciation for “slow travel.” The city’s nickname, “The Summer Capital of China,” is no joke—Guiyang’s mild climate makes it a perfect escape from the scorching heat of other Chinese cities during July and August. Add to that the explosion of Guizhou’s “sour and spicy” cuisine on platforms like Xiaohongshu (Little Red Book), and you have a recipe for a travel boom.

For international travelers, Guiyang is still relatively off the beaten path. That means fewer crowds, lower prices, and a more authentic experience. The local bus system is cheap and efficient, street food stalls serve meals for under 15 RMB (about $2 USD), and many attractions—like Qianling Park or the Jiaxiu Tower—are either free or cost pocket change. The challenge, however, is finding accommodation that doesn’t eat up your entire daily budget. That’s where hostels come in.

What to Look for in a Guiyang Hostel

Not all hostels are created equal. In Guiyang, you’ll find everything from party hostels with rooftop bars to quiet, family-run guesthouses tucked away in old residential neighborhoods. Here are a few factors to consider when choosing where to stay:

  • Location: Guiyang’s public transport is decent, but you’ll want to stay near a metro station or a major bus route. The city center, around the Zhongshan Road and Dashizi areas, is ideal for first-time visitors.
  • Cleanliness: This is non-negotiable. Guiyang is humid, and mold can be an issue in older buildings. Read recent reviews carefully.
  • Social Atmosphere: If you’re traveling solo, look for hostels with common areas, organized activities, or a bar. If you need quiet, check if the hostel has designated quiet hours.
  • Amenities: Free Wi-Fi, laundry service, and luggage storage are standard. Some hostels also offer free breakfast or bike rentals.
  • Price: A good dorm bed in Guiyang should cost between 40 and 80 RMB per night. Private rooms range from 120 to 250 RMB.

Top Hostels in Guiyang for Budget Travelers

1. The Loft Youth Hostel (阁楼青年旅舍)

Location: Near the Dashizi metro station, just a 10-minute walk from the bustling Zhongshan Road pedestrian street.

The Loft is a favorite among backpackers for its industrial-chic design and warm, welcoming vibe. Housed in a converted warehouse, this hostel features high ceilings, exposed brick walls, and large windows that flood the common area with natural light. The dorm beds are sturdy and come with privacy curtains, reading lights, and individual power sockets—a small luxury that makes a big difference after a long day of exploring.

Why it’s great for budget travelers: - Price: Dorm beds start at 45 RMB per night. Private rooms are around 150 RMB. - Community: The Loft hosts weekly movie nights, dumpling-making classes, and city walking tours. It’s easy to find travel buddies here. - Amenities: Free coffee and tea all day, a fully equipped kitchen, and a rooftop terrace with views of the city skyline. - Local tip: The staff can help you arrange day trips to Huangguoshu Waterfall or the Miao villages in Xijiang. They also have a map of the best street food stalls within walking distance.

Potential downsides: The walls are a bit thin, so noise can travel. If you’re a light sleeper, bring earplugs.

2. Guiyang Backpackers Hostel (贵阳背包客青年旅舍)

Location: In the heart of the old town, near the Jiaxiu Tower and the Nanming River.

This is a classic backpacker hostel with a no-frills approach. It’s been around for over a decade and has a loyal following among seasoned travelers. The vibe is casual and unpretentious—think mismatched furniture, a bulletin board covered in travel ads, and a common room where people trade stories over bottles of local beer.

Why it’s great for budget travelers: - Price: Dorm beds are among the cheapest in the city, starting at 38 RMB per night. - Location: You can walk to Jiaxiu Tower, the Riverside Night Market, and several bus stops that connect to the train station and airport. - Social scene: The hostel has a small bar that sells cheap drinks, and the owner often joins guests for cards or mahjong. - Local knowledge: The staff are long-term residents of Guiyang and can give you insider tips on hidden temples, cheap eats, and free attractions.

Potential downsides: The facilities are a bit dated. The showers have inconsistent water pressure, and the Wi-Fi can be spotty during peak hours.

3. Mountain Spring Hostel (山泉青年旅舍)

Location: In the Yunyan District, a 15-minute bus ride from the city center.

If you’re looking for a quieter, more nature-focused stay, Mountain Spring is your best bet. This hostel is set in a converted residential building near Qianling Park, Guiyang’s largest urban green space. The park itself is a must-visit—it’s home to wild monkeys, a beautiful lake, and the Hongfu Temple.

Why it’s great for budget travelers: - Price: Dorm beds are 50 RMB per night. Private rooms with a garden view are 180 RMB. - Atmosphere: The hostel has a Zen-like feel, with a small courtyard garden, a fish pond, and a reading room stocked with travel books. - Amenities: Free bicycle rentals, a communal kitchen, and a laundry service for 10 RMB per load. - Nearby attractions: You can hike in Qianling Park for free, and the hostel is close to the Guizhou Provincial Museum (also free).

Potential downsides: It’s a bit far from the nightlife and street food hotspots. You’ll need to take a bus or taxi to get to the city center.

4. The Wanderers Hostel (行者青年旅舍)

Location: Near the Guiyang Railway Station, making it a convenient stop for travelers arriving by train.

The Wanderers is a newer hostel that has quickly gained a reputation for its modern facilities and friendly staff. The interior is clean and minimalist, with a Scandinavian-inspired design. The dorm rooms are spacious, and the private rooms come with en-suite bathrooms—a rarity at this price point.

Why it’s great for budget travelers: - Price: Dorm beds start at 55 RMB. Private rooms with a double bed are 200 RMB. - Location: The hostel is a 5-minute walk from the railway station, which makes it ideal for onward travel to Kunming, Chengdu, or Guilin. - Amenities: Free high-speed Wi-Fi, a coworking space with desks and monitors, and a rooftop bar that serves craft cocktails for 25 RMB. - Social events: The hostel organizes pub crawls, hotpot dinners, and language exchange nights.

Potential downsides: The area around the railway station can be a bit chaotic, with heavy traffic and street vendors. It’s not the most picturesque part of the city.

5. GreenTree Inn Hostel (绿树青年旅舍)

Location: In the Huaxi District, near the Huaxi Park and the Guizhou University campus.

This hostel is a bit off the tourist trail, but it offers a unique experience for travelers who want to see a different side of Guiyang. Huaxi District is known for its scenic riverside walks, traditional architecture, and slower pace of life. The hostel itself is a converted traditional house with wooden beams, tiled roofs, and a peaceful garden.

Why it’s great for budget travelers: - Price: Dorm beds are 40 RMB per night. Private rooms are 130 RMB. - Atmosphere: It’s quiet, clean, and feels more like a homestay than a hostel. The owner lives on-site and often cooks breakfast for guests. - Nearby attractions: Huaxi Park is a 10-minute walk away, and the Qingyan Ancient Town is a short bus ride from the hostel. - Local food: The Huaxi District is famous for its street food, especially the “Huaxi beef noodles” and grilled skewers.

Potential downsides: The hostel is not near a metro station, and buses can be infrequent. You’ll need to plan your trips carefully.

Budget Travel Tips for Guiyang

Staying in a hostel is just one part of the budget travel equation. Here are a few extra tips to stretch your money further in Guiyang:

Eat Like a Local

Guiyang’s street food is legendary, and it’s incredibly cheap. Don’t miss: - Sour and Fish Soup (酸汤鱼): A local specialty that costs around 30 RMB per person at a street stall. - Changwang Noodles (肠旺面): A spicy noodle soup with pork intestines and blood tofu. About 12 RMB per bowl. - Grilled Tofu (烤豆腐): Served with a dipping sauce made from chili, garlic, and Sichuan pepper. A plate costs 5 RMB. - Rice Tofu (米豆腐): A cold, jelly-like dish served with chili oil and peanuts. Perfect for hot days.

Use Public Transport

Guiyang’s metro system has two lines that cover most major attractions. A single ride costs between 2 and 6 RMB. Buses are even cheaper, at 1 to 2 RMB per ride. Taxis are affordable too, but avoid taking them during rush hour when traffic is heavy.

Visit Free Attractions

Many of Guiyang’s best sights are free or nearly free: - Qianling Park: Free entry. You can spend an entire day hiking, watching monkeys, and visiting the temple. - Jiaxiu Tower: Entry is 5 RMB. It’s a small but iconic pavilion on the Nanming River. - Guizhou Provincial Museum: Free. The exhibits on Miao and Dong minority cultures are fascinating. - Huaxi Park: Free. A beautiful spot for a picnic or a leisurely walk.

Book in Advance During Peak Season

Guiyang’s popularity has grown, especially during the summer months (June to August) and Chinese national holidays (October 1-7). Hostels can fill up quickly, so book at least a week in advance. Use platforms like Booking.com, Hostelworld, or Ctrip to compare prices and read reviews.

The Rise of “Hostel Tourism” in Guizhou

One interesting trend in 2025 is the rise of “hostel tourism” in Guizhou province. More and more travelers are choosing to stay in hostels not just for the price, but for the experience. Hostels in Guiyang are increasingly offering curated experiences—like hiking trips to the Longgong Caves, cooking classes for local dishes, and even volunteer opportunities at minority village schools.

This shift is partly driven by social media. On platforms like Instagram and TikTok, travelers share videos of their hostel stays—rooftop views, group dinners, and impromptu jam sessions. These posts have turned hostels into destinations in their own right. The Loft Youth Hostel, for example, has become a hotspot for Instagrammers because of its photogenic interiors and sunset views from the terrace.

Another factor is the growing interest in sustainable travel. Many Guiyang hostels are adopting eco-friendly practices, such as using solar water heaters, offering refillable water stations, and partnering with local farmers for food supplies. For budget travelers who care about their environmental footprint, these hostels are a natural choice.

Final Thoughts on Hostels in Guiyang

Guiyang is a city that rewards the curious traveler. It’s not flashy or polished, but it has a raw, authentic energy that’s hard to find in more touristy destinations. The hostels here reflect that spirit—they’re affordable, friendly, and full of character. Whether you choose the industrial charm of The Loft, the old-school vibe of Guiyang Backpackers, or the quiet retreat of Mountain Spring, you’ll find a home base that lets you explore the city without breaking the bank.

Remember to pack light, bring an appetite for spicy food, and leave some room in your itinerary for spontaneous adventures. Guiyang has a way of surprising you. And with the right hostel, those surprises become stories you’ll tell for years.
