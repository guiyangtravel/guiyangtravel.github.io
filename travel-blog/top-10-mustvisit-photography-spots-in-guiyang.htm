Top 10 Must-Visit Photography Spots in Guiyang

  1. Home
  2. Travel Blog
  3. Blog Details

Guiyang, the capital of Guizhou province, is a destination that consistently defies expectations. Nestled among rolling green hills and often shrouded in ethereal mist, it’s a city where modern vitality meets ancient tranquility. For photographers, it’s a dream—a canvas painted with dramatic karst landscapes, vibrant ethnic culture, futuristic architecture, and pockets of profound serenity. This guide will take you through the top 10 photography spots in Guiyang, ensuring you capture the essence of this captivating city.

The Natural Wonders: Where Karst Meets Water

Guiyang’s surrounding landscape is the star of the show, offering breathtaking backdrops that are both majestic and intimate.

Qianling Mountain Park

This urban oasis is the green lung of Guiyang and a fantastic starting point. The park is centered around Qianling Lake and is home to hundreds of semi-wild macaque monkeys, offering incredible opportunities for wildlife and environmental portraiture. For the iconic shot, hike up to the Hongfu Temple. The climb through lush forest, with glimpses of the city below, is photogenic itself. At the top, you can capture the temple’s intricate architecture against the skyline or frame a monk’s silhouette against an ancient doorway. Early morning is magical, as mist rises from the lake and the first light filters through the trees, creating a soft, mystical atmosphere.

Qingyan Ancient Town

Step back in time just an hour from the city center. Qingyan is not just an ancient town; it’s a living museum of Ming and Qing dynasty architecture. Your lens will be drawn to the towering city gates, narrow, flagstone streets, and centuries-old stone courtyards. The key here is detail and storytelling. Focus on the textures of the moss-covered walls, the hanging red lanterns contrasting with grey slate roofs, and the daily life of vendors selling local snacks like changwangmian (hot noodles) and gaoba (sticky rice cake). Visit on a weekday to avoid crowds and capture cleaner compositions. The interplay of light and shadow in the late afternoon along its alleyways is particularly dramatic.

Huaxi National Urban Wetland Park

A favorite among local photographers, this park showcases Guiyang’s commitment to ecological harmony. It’s a vast area of rivers, wetlands, and farmland. The Ten-Li Riverfront corridor is perfect for long-exposure shots of the calm water reflecting the willows and distant hills. In different seasons, it offers varied palettes: golden canola flowers in spring, lush green rice paddies in summer, and romantic reed beds in autumn. For a unique perspective, rent a bicycle and explore the winding paths, capturing candid moments of locals fishing or farmers tending to their fields.

The Cultural Heartbeat: Ethnic Villages & Urban Spirit

The soul of Guiyang is found in the traditions of its people and the rhythm of its urban life.

Guiyang Olympic Sports Center & The "Dinosaurs"

For a stark contrast to the ancient sites, head to the Guiyang Olympic Sports Center. Its main stadium, with a design inspired by the region’s iconic lusheng (a reed-pipe instrument), is a masterpiece of modern architecture. The real photographic hotspot, however, is the nearby "Four Dragons Holding a Pearl" building—often humorously nicknamed the "Dinosaur Building" by locals and visitors. Its futuristic, skeletal structure is a gift for architectural photographers. Shoot at blue hour, when the building’s lights turn on against the deep blue twilight sky, creating a stunning sci-fi aesthetic.

Zhenshan Ethnic Village

Located on the peninsula of Huaxi Lake, Zhenshan is a well-preserved village of the Buyi ethnic minority. It’s a cultural photography treasure trove. Capture the traditional stone-and-wood stilt houses built along the hillside, the colorful embroidered costumes of the local women, and the intricate silver jewelry. If you’re lucky to visit during a festival, you might witness lusheng dances or traditional singing. Portraiture here, done with respect and often a friendly smile, can yield powerfully evocative images that tell a story of enduring heritage.

Jiaxiu Pavilion

This is Guiyang’s most iconic landmark. Standing proudly on Fuyu Bridge over the Nanming River, the three-story Jiaxiu Pavilion is a symbol of the city’s literary history. The classic shot is from across the river, capturing its elegant reflection in the water, ideally at night when it’s brilliantly lit. Don’t stop there. Walk across the bridge for detailed shots of the pavilion’s eaves and pillars. The surrounding area, with locals dancing, practicing tai chi, or simply strolling, offers fantastic environmental shots that place this ancient structure firmly within the pulse of modern city life.

The Hidden Gems & Panoramic Vistas

Beyond the famous sites lie quieter places that offer unique perspectives and unforgettable views.

Baihua Lake

A short drive from the urban core, Baihua Lake feels like a different world. It’s one of Guizhou’s largest artificial lakes, surrounded by forested karst peaks. The scale here is grand. Take a boat trip to capture the vast expanse of water dotted with hundreds of islands. The "Three Brothers Peak" rising from the lake is a famous subject. Aerial photography (where permitted) shines here, revealing the stunning patterns of islands and emerald-green water. It’s a location for landscape purists, offering serenity and grandeur.

Guiyang Confucius Temple (Wenmiao)

Tucked away in the bustling city center, this quiet temple complex is an oasis of calm and classical beauty. Built during the Ming Dynasty, it’s a superb spot for architectural detail. Focus on the majestic pailou (decorated archway), the serene courtyards with ancient cypress trees, and the ornate carvings on the Dacheng Hall. The symmetry and bold colors—deep reds, greens, and golds—against the grey stone are visually striking. It’s less crowded than other sites, allowing for contemplative photography focused on form, color, and quiet ambiance.

Qianchun Ancient Town

Often overlooked in favor of Qingyan, Qianchun offers a more rustic, "unrestored" atmosphere. Located within the Huaxi Wetland Park area, this Ming Dynasty town is quieter and lets you photograph history in a raw state. The crumbling walls, old wooden doors with rusted locks, and quiet lanes tell a story of the passage of time. It’s perfect for photographers seeking a moody, documentary, or historical aesthetic. The contrast between the decaying structures and the vibrant green nature reclaiming spaces is profoundly photographic.

Xiangzhimen & The City Skyline

For the definitive panoramic view of modern Guiyang, make your way to the observation decks around Xiangzhimen (Rhododendron Gate) and the surrounding hillside parks. As the sun sets and the city lights begin to twinkle, you’ll get a breathtaking vista of Guiyang’s forest of skyscrapers nestled in a bowl of hills. Use a telephoto lens to compress the cityscape or a wide-angle to capture the sweeping view. This spot encapsulates the "Cloud City" nickname, especially when mist settles between the buildings, creating a layered, dynamic image of a city soaring from an ancient landscape.
