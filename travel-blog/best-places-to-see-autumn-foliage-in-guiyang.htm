Best Places to See Autumn Foliage in Guiyang

  1. Home
  2. Travel Blog
  3. Blog Details

When autumn arrives in Guiyang, the capital of Guizhou Province, the city transforms into a living canvas of amber, crimson, and gold. Unlike the more famous fall foliage destinations in northern China or Japan, Guiyang offers a unique subtropical twist: the colors are softer, the air is cooler, and the crowds are thinner. For travelers seeking an off-the-beaten-path autumn experience, Guiyang is a hidden gem that rewards slow exploration. In this guide, I’ll walk you through the best spots to witness autumn foliage in Guiyang, along with practical tips on timing, photography, and local culture. Whether you’re a nature lover, a photographer, or just someone who wants to escape the urban grind, these places will leave you breathless.

Why Guiyang’s Autumn Foliage Is Special

Guiyang sits at an elevation of about 1,100 meters (3,600 feet), giving it a mild, humid climate that extends the autumn season well into November. The city’s nickname, "Forest City," is no exaggeration—parks, hills, and lakes are woven into the urban fabric. But what makes Guiyang’s fall colors stand out is the diversity of trees. You’ll find ginkgo biloba, maple, sweetgum, and metasequoia (dawn redwood) all changing color in a staggered rhythm. The ginkgo trees, in particular, turn a brilliant, almost neon yellow that glows against the gray limestone mountains. Add to that the misty mornings and clear, crisp afternoons, and you have a photographer’s paradise.

Qianling Park: The Crown Jewel of Autumn Colors

If you only have time for one spot, make it Qianling Park (Qianling Shan Gongyuan). This sprawling urban park, located in the northwestern part of the city, is a mix of forested hills, temples, and a zoo. But in autumn, the real star is the Ginkgo Avenue.

The Ginkgo Avenue Experience

As you enter the park’s main gate, follow the path that leads uphill toward the Hongfu Temple. About halfway up, you’ll encounter a straight stretch of road lined with towering ginkgo trees. In late October to mid-November, these trees drop their leaves, carpeting the ground in a thick layer of gold. The contrast between the yellow leaves and the red walls of the temple creates a classic Chinese aesthetic that feels straight out of a Tang dynasty painting. Locals come here to practice tai chi, play badminton, or simply sit on benches and watch the leaves fall. For the best photos, arrive early in the morning (around 7:00 AM) when the light is soft and the crowds are sparse.

Hiking to the Summit

For a more immersive experience, take the hiking trail to the top of Qianling Mountain. The trail winds through mixed forests of maple, pine, and camphor, and the autumn colors here are more subtle—think burnt orange and deep burgundy rather than bright red. The payoff at the summit is a panoramic view of Guiyang’s skyline, with the golden treetops spreading out below like a patchwork quilt. On clear days, you can see the distant mountains of the Guizhou Plateau. This hike takes about 1.5 hours round trip, so pack water and wear comfortable shoes.

Huaxi National Wetland Park: A Riverside Autumn Dream

About 12 kilometers south of the city center, Huaxi National Wetland Park is a sprawling network of rivers, lakes, and bamboo groves. While it’s beautiful year-round, autumn brings a special charm to the waterways.

The Golden Bamboo and Metasequoia Forest

One of the park’s highlights is the metasequoia forest near the main lake. These deciduous conifers turn a warm, russet brown in fall, and their needle-like leaves create a soft texture that feels almost fuzzy. Walk along the wooden boardwalks that crisscross the wetland, and you’ll see the reflections of these trees in the still water. The contrast between the brown leaves and the green moss on the rocks is a photographer’s dream.

Cycling Through the Color

The park is huge, so renting a bicycle is a great way to cover more ground. There are dedicated bike paths that loop around the lake and through the bamboo groves. In autumn, the bamboo remains green, but the undergrowth of ferns and shrubs turns yellow and orange, creating a layered effect. Stop at the small bridges and pavilions to take in the view. If you’re lucky, you might spot locals fishing or families having picnics under the trees.

Jiaxiu Tower and the Nanming River: Urban Fall Foliage

For a more urban autumn experience, head to Jiaxiu Tower, the iconic landmark of Guiyang. Located on a small island in the Nanming River, this Ming-dynasty tower is surrounded by weeping willows, ginkgo trees, and flowering shrubs.

The Riverside Walk

The promenade along the Nanming River, from Jiaxiu Tower to the nearby Wenchang Pavilion, is lined with ginkgo and sweetgum trees. In autumn, the leaves turn a mix of yellow and orange, and the reflections in the river create a double exposure effect. The best time to visit is late afternoon, when the golden light hits the tower’s eaves and the leaves seem to glow. This area is popular with local photographers, so expect to see tripods and long lenses.

Night Photography

Don’t leave after sunset. Jiaxiu Tower is beautifully illuminated at night, and the autumn foliage takes on a magical quality under the warm lights. The contrast between the dark river, the lit tower, and the golden leaves is stunning. Bring a tripod and experiment with long exposures to capture the blurred motion of the water.

Tianhe Lake: A Hidden Gem for Solitude

About 30 kilometers northeast of the city, Tianhe Lake (Tianhe Hu) is a reservoir surrounded by forested hills. It’s less crowded than Qianling Park or Huaxi Wetland, making it ideal for those seeking solitude.

The Lakeside Trail

A 10-kilometer trail circles the lake, passing through stands of maple, oak, and pine. In autumn, the maples turn a deep, vibrant red, while the oaks go yellow and brown. The trail is mostly flat, so it’s suitable for all fitness levels. Along the way, you’ll find small pavilions and benches where you can sit and watch the leaves drift onto the water. The lake itself is a deep blue-green, and the reflections of the autumn colors on the surface are mesmerizing.

Boating and Birdwatching

If you want to get closer to the foliage, rent a rowboat or pedal boat from the dock. Paddling out onto the lake gives you a different perspective—the trees on the shore look like a wall of fire, and the silence is broken only by the sound of oars. Keep an eye out for migratory birds, such as egrets and herons, which stop at the lake during their southward journey.

Gaopo Village and the Ancient Ginkgo Trees

For a truly unique autumn experience, take a day trip to Gaopo Village (Gaopo Miao Xiang), about 60 kilometers southwest of Guiyang. This Miao ethnic village is famous for its ancient ginkgo trees, some of which are over 1,000 years old.

The Millennium Ginkgo Trees

The village is home to a cluster of giant ginkgo trees, with trunks so wide that it takes three or four people to wrap their arms around them. In autumn, these trees turn a brilliant yellow, and the ground is covered in a thick carpet of leaves. The Miao villagers, who wear traditional embroidered clothing, often gather under the trees to dry chili peppers or husk rice. The scene is a photographer’s dream—ancient trees, colorful ethnic costumes, and golden leaves.

Cultural Immersion

Gaopo Village is not just about the foliage. You can also visit the local Miao homes, which are built from wood and stone, and sample traditional dishes like sour fish soup and sticky rice. The villagers are friendly and used to visitors, so don’t hesitate to ask for a photo or a story. If you’re interested in handicrafts, look for handmade batik fabrics and silver jewelry.

Practical Tips for Autumn Foliage Viewing in Guiyang

Timing Is Everything

The autumn foliage season in Guiyang typically runs from late October to early December, with the peak colors occurring in mid-November. However, the exact timing depends on the weather. A cold snap can accelerate the color change, while a warm spell can delay it. Check local travel forums or social media for real-time updates before you go.

What to Pack

  • Clothing: Guiyang’s autumn weather is unpredictable. Mornings and evenings can be cool (10-15°C / 50-59°F), while afternoons can be warm (20-25°C / 68-77°F). Layering is key. Bring a light jacket, a sweater, and comfortable walking shoes.
  • Gear: A camera with a zoom lens is essential for capturing the foliage. A polarizing filter can help reduce glare from the water and enhance the colors. Don’t forget a tripod for low-light shots.
  • Other Essentials: Sunscreen, insect repellent, and a reusable water bottle. The air in Guiyang is generally clean, but autumn can bring occasional haze.

Getting Around

Guiyang has a good public transportation system, including buses and a metro line. For outlying spots like Tianhe Lake and Gaopo Village, consider renting a car or joining a guided tour. Didi (the Chinese Uber) is also widely available and affordable.

Food and Drinks

Autumn is harvest season in Guizhou, so you’ll find seasonal specialties like chestnuts, persimmons, and pomegranates at street stalls. After a day of foliage viewing, warm up with a bowl of Guiyang’s famous spicy beef noodles (Guiyang niu rou fen) or a cup of hot sour plum soup. For a truly local experience, try the fermented rice wine (mi jiu) at a Miao village.

Photography Tips for Autumn Foliage

Composition

  • Leading Lines: Use paths, rivers, or tree trunks to draw the viewer’s eye into the frame.
  • Reflections: Still water is your best friend. Look for lakes or ponds that reflect the autumn colors.
  • Foreground Interest: Include a rock, a leaf, or a person in the foreground to add depth.

Camera Settings

  • Aperture: Use a wide aperture (f/2.8 to f/4) for portraits or close-ups of leaves, and a narrow aperture (f/8 to f/11) for landscapes.
  • ISO: Keep ISO low (100-400) to avoid noise. If you’re shooting in low light, use a tripod instead of cranking up the ISO.
  • White Balance: Set your white balance to "Cloudy" or "Shade" to warm up the colors.

Etiquette

  • Respect the Environment: Don’t pick leaves or branches. Stay on designated paths to avoid damaging the roots.
  • Respect the Locals: If you’re photographing people, always ask for permission first. A smile and a nod go a long way.

Beyond the Foliage: Autumn Events in Guiyang

If you’re visiting Guiyang in autumn, you’re in luck—the city hosts several cultural events that complement the foliage season.

The Guiyang International Music Festival

Held in late October, this festival brings together musicians from China and abroad for concerts in venues like the Guiyang Grand Theater and Qianling Park. The combination of live music and autumn colors is unforgettable.

The Miao New Year

The Miao ethnic group celebrates their New Year in November, with festivals in villages like Gaopo and Xijiang. Expect traditional dances, bullfights, and feasts. The timing coincides with the peak foliage, so you can enjoy both nature and culture in one trip.

The Guiyang Food Festival

Autumn is also the season for the Guiyang Food Festival, held in the city center. Sample local specialties like sour fish, spicy hotpot, and bamboo rice. The festival is a great way to warm up after a day in the cool air.

Final Thoughts on Guiyang’s Autumn Foliage

Guiyang may not be the first place that comes to mind when you think of autumn foliage, but that’s precisely its charm. The city offers a quieter, more intimate experience compared to the crowded hotspots in Japan or New England. The combination of subtropical forests, ancient ginkgo trees, and ethnic culture creates a tapestry that is uniquely Guizhou. Whether you’re hiking through Qianling Park, cycling around Huaxi Wetland, or sipping tea under a millennium-old ginkgo tree in Gaopo Village, you’ll find that Guiyang’s autumn is a season to be savored slowly.

So pack your camera, put on your walking shoes, and get ready to fall in love with Guiyang’s golden season. The leaves are waiting.
